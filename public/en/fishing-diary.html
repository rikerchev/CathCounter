Fishing diary: what to record and why

A well-kept diary is the cheapest way to start catching better. After about ten trips you begin to see patterns that memory alone does not keep.

Why keep a fishing diary

You remember the big fish, but you forget exactly what they took, at what time and in what weather. A diary brings those details back. It helps you work out which spots, baits and groundbaits deliver results, so you don't repeat trips that didn't work.

What is worth recording

  • Date and time of the catch. The time shows when the fish bite — in the morning, at midday or in the evening.
  • Water body and exact spot. The same reservoir can be good on one side and poor on the other.
  • Fish species and weight. Personal bests and average weight by species.
  • Bait and groundbait. The main and the second groundbait, if you use two.
  • Hook, leader and casting distance. Small details often decide the bite.
  • Conditions. Air temperature, wind and cloud cover. It is also useful to note pressure changes.
  • Photo. A keepsake and proof of the size.

How to do it quickly on the bank

A diary is most often abandoned because logging takes time and your hands are wet. In CatchCount the flow is built for the bank: you tap “Start Timer” when you cast, and “Land Fish” when you land a fish. The timer measures how much time has passed, and the catch form is pre-filled with the rod's settings — bait, groundbait, hook and distance. All that is left is to mark the species and weight. The location is picked up automatically.

If you fish with several rods, each one has its own timer and its own settings. You can set a reminder with a sound alert if you want to change your bait at a set interval.

How to read it afterwards

After every trip the session is summarized: number of fish, total weight, biggest fish, average temperature and wind, number of casts. That way you see not only how much you caught, but also how much effort it took. Over time, the statistics show which baits, groundbaits and spots work best for you.

Tips for a more useful diary

  • Log the trips with no catch too — they show where and when the fish don't bite.
  • Be consistent with the names of your baits and groundbaits so they group correctly.
  • Don't try to draw conclusions after a single trip. Patterns show up with more data.
  • Keep logging your catches without internet too — the app syncs them later.
